# 18.C25: Learning Hard Things in the Age of AI

Fall 2026 course page, under construction.

This year we are rethinking 18.C25 as a project laboratory. The class will still use Julia and Pluto notebooks, but the center of gravity is shifting from "learn to program" toward "learn something hard, make the learning visible, and teach it to other people."

Students will choose a topic that feels a little out of reach, narrow it to one idea they can actually teach, build an interactive Pluto notebook, and present it to the class. The notebook should be something another student can run, poke, vary, question, and learn from.

## Fall 2026 Direction

LLMs are now part of the intellectual landscape. They are useful, they are fallible, and they will get better. This course begins from that fact, but it will not confuse a fluent machine-generated answer with human understanding.

There will be two ways through the course:

- **AI-assisted track:** use LLMs as tutors, critics, code assistants, and generators of examples, while checking important claims against serious sources.
- **No-LLM track:** use books, lecture notes, papers, classmates, office hours, and computation, but not LLMs for explanation, writing, or code.

The standard is the same for both tracks: learn something real, build something that teaches, and answer questions in the room.

## The Work

The current plan is for students to:

- study old 18.C25 notebooks as examples of computational communication;
- remix an old notebook to make it teach better;
- choose an ambitious topic and narrow it to one teachable idea;
- keep a learning journal showing questions, sources, false starts, and changes of mind;
- build an interactive Pluto notebook;
- present the notebook and defend the explanation in class.

Possible project slices include:

- geodesics as straightest possible paths;
- the Lie bracket as failure to commute;
- entropy increase in a toy gas model;
- parallel transport on a sphere;
- Noether's theorem in one simple mechanical system;
- what a tensor is, and why coordinates are annoying.

## First Assignments

These are provisional.

1. **Notebook X-Ray.** Choose one old 18.C25 Pluto notebook and take it apart. What is the main idea? What does the notebook let you see or try that a static page would not? What questions does it raise?
2. **Remix An Old Notebook.** Make a meaningful improvement to an old notebook: add an experiment, simplify a confusing section, make a better picture, add a counterexample, or turn a passive explanation into something the reader can manipulate.
3. **The Impossible Topic Proposal.** Pick the hard thing. Not all of general relativity or all of thermodynamics, but one slice you might plausibly teach by the end of the semester.
